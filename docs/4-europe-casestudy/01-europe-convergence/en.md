# European convergence: is the catch-up real?

Two hundred years ago a worker in Lisbon earned a fraction of what a worker in Munich earned. Today the gap is much smaller, and the standard hope is that economies starting lower simply grow faster until they meet the leaders. Here x = GDP per capita in 2000, y = average growth 2000–24; one point per EU member (18 of the EU's 27 countries shown).

**Judgment**: convergence holds overall — Eastern Europe (Poland, Romania, Slovakia) started low and grew 4–5% a year, steadily catching up. But the left-hand group splits: Greece and Italy sit on the same low-growth band, and convergence cannot explain the southern stagnation. That one is a story about structure and debt, not catch-up.
