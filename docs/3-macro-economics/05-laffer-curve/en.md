# Laffer curve: the mountain nobody finds

Your boss promises to stop docking your pay if the company hires more. You raise your eyebrows: less pay, more jobs — where does the money come from? That is the Laffer curve in one story. It says tax cuts can pay for themselves, because past some rate, charging more shrinks what people earn, hide, or bring home. To draw it properly you need **tax rates** (the knob you turn). Public databases mostly carry **revenue shares** (the outcome you get). So this chart uses what is on offer: x = government revenue as a share of GDP (2000–25 average), y = average real growth over the same years, one point per G20 economy.

**Judgment**: there is **no visible mountain** anywhere between Saudi Arabia's ~20% and France's ~45%. Low-tax Saudi Arabia and Mexico sit in the same growth band as high-tax France and Germany. The Laffer mountain does not show up in real data: what moves growth is institutions, skills and the business cycle, and all three dwarf the revenue share. The curve may well exist at extreme rates — real economies just never live at the extremes.
