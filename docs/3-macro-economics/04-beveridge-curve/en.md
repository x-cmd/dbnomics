# Beveridge curve: vacancies vs unemployment

The Beveridge curve links two numbers that usually move in opposite directions: job vacancies (posts nobody has filled) and unemployment (people without a job). Many vacancies plus low unemployment is a boom; the reverse is a slump. When the curve shifts outward, the two stop matching — jobs sit empty while people stay unemployed.

**Judgment**: the blue path 2001–2019 slides along one stable curve, down in crises and up in expansions. The red path 2020–24 is the textbook case: the pandemic shoved the curve **outward** — vacancies above 7% in 2021–22 while unemployment fell (the Great Resignation broke matching) — and then in 2023–24 vacancies fell without unemployment rising much, i.e. normalisation along a new, higher curve rather than a return to the old one. That leftover outward shift is the pandemic's lasting scar on the US labour market.
