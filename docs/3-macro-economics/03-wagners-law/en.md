# Wagner's law: why governments keep growing

Wagner's law, named after a 19th-century German economist: as people get richer they want more public services, so government takes a bigger share of the economy. Here x = GDP per capita, y = government revenue as a share of GDP; each path runs left to right through time.

**Judgment**: China is the textbook case — per-capita GDP from $951 (2000) to $12,969 (2024) while the revenue share doubled from 13.4% to 26.4%: urbanisation, social insurance and infrastructure all swelling with income. The US path is nearly vertical — already rich, share flat around 30%. Government size converges in rich economies, which is the second half of Wagner's story.
