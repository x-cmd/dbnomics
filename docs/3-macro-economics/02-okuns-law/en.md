# Okun's law: how growth buys jobs

When a factory in your city switches to night shifts and starts hiring, does the whole neighbourhood notice? Okun's law says yes, and it says by roughly how much. The rule is a rule of thumb with a long track record. When growth runs below its normal rate, unemployment rises by roughly half as much. Here x = real growth, y = the year-on-year change in unemployment. Points to the lower right mean growth eating joblessness, points to the upper left mean recession pushing it up.

**Judgment**: the US cloud hugs a clean downward slope, and it held for 25 years. The 2010s expansion chewed through unemployment with 2%+ growth. The only two points off the line are 2020–21, and those are the lockdown switch being flipped rather than the law failing. China's cloud shifts up and to the right. The link between growth and jobs is weaker there, because a country in structural transition stops turning growth into headcount.

The euro area's slope is flatter: rigid labour markets cushion the link between growth and jobs. Which is also why Europe can grow a little and lose a little unemployment at the same time.
