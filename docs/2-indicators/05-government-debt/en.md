# Government debt

Picture a country's bills stacked on a kitchen table. Government debt as a share of GDP is how much of what a country produces in a year is already promised to someone else. The **Maastricht** reference of 60% — the EU's guideline for euro-area members — is a warning light on the dashboard, not a wall. Cross it and nothing happens on the spot. Ignore it for years, and the road gets expensive.

**Judgment**: what decides whether the bill is affordable is r − g. That is the gap between the interest rate you pay (r) and the rate the economy grows (g). Pay less interest than the economy earns, and the ratio drifts down even while you keep spending. Pay more, and the debt compounds faster than the country grows. Japan sits near 250% of GDP and stays stable, because the debt is held by domestic savers at low rates. Greece is the crisis case. The US is above 120% and still climbing, in peacetime. The red line on the chart is the 60% reference. Of the fourteen economies shown, only three still sit under it.
