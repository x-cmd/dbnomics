# Government debt

Government debt as a share of GDP is the headline number for whether a government can keep going. The **Maastricht** reference of 60% is a guideline the EU set for euro-area members — a warning light, not a hard wall.

What decides sustainability is r − g: if the interest rate you pay (r) is below the growth rate of the economy (g), the ratio drifts down even while you keep spending; if r > g, debt compounds faster than the economy grows. Japan sits near 250% of GDP and stays stable — the debt is held by domestic savers at low rates. Greece is the crisis case. The US is above 120% and still climbing in peacetime.
