# The current account

The current account adds up what a country sells to the rest of the world minus what it buys from it, plus the income and transfers earned abroad — as a share of GDP. A surplus means the country is a net lender to the world; a deficit means it is a net borrower.

A surplus is not a trophy and a deficit is not a disease — both are just the mirror of the gap between what a country saves and what it invests. Germany's 6%+ surplus is a friction point inside the euro area (the money keeps pooling in one corner); China settled at 1–2%; the US runs a ~3% deficit and can finance it indefinitely because it borrows in its own currency. The red bars show which side each economy is on.
