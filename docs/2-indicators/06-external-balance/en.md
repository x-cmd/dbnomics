# The current account

Every shop in your city sells something made abroad, and every wage you earn buys something made abroad. The current account is the running tally of that trade. It adds up what a country sells to the rest of the world minus what it buys from it. Add the income and transfers earned abroad, and express the whole thing as a share of GDP. A surplus means the country ends up lending to the world. A deficit means it ends up borrowing.

**Judgment**: a surplus is not a trophy and a deficit is not a disease. Both are just the mirror of the gap between what a country saves and what it invests. Germany's 6%+ surplus is a friction point inside the euro area, where the money keeps pooling in one corner. China settled at 1–2%. The US runs a ~3% deficit and can finance it indefinitely, because it borrows in its own currency. The red bars in the chart are the deficits. The US, Russia, and Saudi A. are on that side.
